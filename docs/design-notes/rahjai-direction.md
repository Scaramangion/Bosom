Absolutely. Then I’d shift Rahjai away from “farm-defense game” and toward a warm, slow-life farming RPG, with the wolves/raids becoming an occasional consequence rather than the main loop.

Think Harvest Moon: A Wonderful Life as the emotional target, while keeping Rahjai’s own 2.5D adventure identity.

The new Rahjai feeling

Morning

* Wake up.
* Check crops and animals.
* Water/harvest.
* Feed animals.
* Walk into town.
* Talk to somebody you’ve gotten to know.
* Maybe give them something.
* Stop at Brennan’s Theme.
* Ride your horse home.

Afternoon

* Work the farm.
* Explore the surrounding land.
* Fish.
* Gather herbs, fruit, mushrooms, etc.
* Find something unusual.
* Visit a neighbor.
* Discover a little piece of someone’s story.

Evening

* Sell your harvest.
* Cook or craft.
* Check the animals.
* Watch the sunset.
* Talk to someone before heading home.

Night

* Sleep.
* The next day arrives.

And then occasionally:

Something is wrong in the fields.

The wolves appear.

Not every night. Night should usually be peaceful.

⸻

Make the farm about LIFE, not defense

Instead of:

“I need to protect my crops tonight.”

I’d make the feeling:

“I wonder what my farm will look like five years from now.”

That’s much closer to A Wonderful Life.

Your farm could gradually develop:

Year 1

* Tiny field
* Few animals
* Small house
* Basic crops
* You barely know anybody

Year 2

* Better tools
* More animals
* More crops
* New buildings
* NPC relationships deepen
* People start remembering things you’ve done

Year 3

* Farm feels established
* Animals have histories
* Certain NPCs have changed
* Old conversations take on new meaning
* New areas become accessible

And importantly, time should matter.

Not because you’re racing.

Because the world is alive.

⸻

Animals should be a huge part of it

This is where Rahjai could really capture that Wonderful Life feeling.

Instead of animals being inventory objects:

Cow #17: +milk

Give them personalities.

A cow might:

* Follow you around.
* Get excited when you approach.
* Refuse to leave the barn.
* Have favorite food.
* Become attached to another animal.
* Produce better milk when cared for.
* Eventually become an old animal you remember.

Same with:

🐄 Cows
🐑 Sheep
🐔 Chickens
🐎 Horses
🐕 Dogs
🐈 Cats

And your horse should be particularly important because Rahjai already has your mounted exploration system.

⸻

Crops should become beautiful

I’d also reduce the feeling of:

plant → wait → harvest → sell

and increase:

I am actually tending this little piece of land.

For example:

Sunroot

Day 1 — tiny shoot
Day 2 — green leaves
Day 3 — flowering
Day 4 — mature

Different weather affects them.

Rain means you don’t have to water.

Too much rain could create weeds.

Good soil improves yield.

Different seasons favor different crops.

Eventually:

Seed → crop → cooking → meal → gift → relationship

Now farming touches the rest of the game.

⸻

And NPCs become the real progression

This is probably the biggest change I’d make.

Don’t make the player’s primary progression:

coins → upgrades → stronger equipment

Make it:

people → memories → relationships → changes in the village

A farmer remembers that you helped them.

A child grows older.

An old merchant changes what they sell.

Someone who used to sit alone eventually joins everyone at Brennan’s Theme.

A character leaves Rahjai for a while and comes back.

A festival happens.

Someone gets married.

Someone has a baby.

Someone dies.

The town changes.

That’s where the “A Wonderful Life” feeling comes from.

⸻

Your existing Brennan’s Theme is perfect for this

You already established it as a Tellhouse where people drink, talk and exchange information.

Make it the social heartbeat.

You walk in and don’t necessarily have a quest.

You might just hear:

“You hear about Old Man ____?”

Or someone remembers:

“You brought me those Emberwheat seeds last spring.”

Or two NPCs are having an argument.

Or somebody tells you about a place you’ve never visited.

Sometimes nothing important happens at all.

That’s important.

The world shouldn’t constantly be giving the player objectives.

Sometimes you’re just living there.

⸻

Keep the wolves—but demote them

I wouldn’t delete your wolf system.

I’d transform it.

Instead of:

EVERY NIGHT = RAID

make it:

The wilderness occasionally pushes back.

Maybe wolves normally stay outside the farm.

But certain conditions can attract them:

* Leaving crops unattended
* Certain seasons
* Carrying meat
* Disturbing a den
* A particular story event
* A bad weather night
* A rare “wolf moon”
* Something supernatural happening in Rahjai

Then one night you’re sitting outside your house…

HOWL.

Your dog gets up.

The chickens start making noise.

You look toward the field.

Three pairs of eyes.

Now the combat actually means something because this is your home.

⸻

The fundamental change I’d make

Right now your design sounds like:

FARM → DEFEND → FARM → DEFEND

I’d turn it into:

LIVE → FARM → EXPLORE → SOCIALIZE → GROW → REMEMBER

with occasional:

“Oh no.”

That’s much closer to the emotional DNA you’re describing.

And it fits Rahjai extremely well because the world you’ve already built has horses, villages, NPC conversations, wilderness, exploration, farming, strange creatures, and a larger fantasy mystery. The farm can become the quiet home that makes the enormous world outside feel meaningful.
